Studio 01 · Constructing the Past

From Canvas to Commodity

Drawing on artists as the core community, this transect illustration seeks to uncover the life cycle of artists moving into an ‘undesirable’ urban space, creating vibrant culture, and being priced out as a reflection of the overall trend of ‘creative eviction’ found throughout New York—and beyond.

Research Process

01

Reading Change through Preservation

Comparing Greenwich Village, Hudson Square, and Tribeca revealed how preservation and redevelopment can retain physical form while transforming local communities and economies. This raised a central question: how does urban change create value, and who is displaced in the process?

02

Finding Artists as the Through-line

Artists revealed a recurring pattern across the transect: they moved into affordable spaces, generated cultural value, and were later priced out as neighborhoods became more desirable. Their movement connected neighborhood identity directly to displacement.

03

Mapping Creative Eviction across Time and Space

The final transect maps artist communities across the three neighborhoods and connects their displacement to changing urban conditions. It frames this process as creative eviction: the city preserves the image of creativity while excluding the communities that produced it.

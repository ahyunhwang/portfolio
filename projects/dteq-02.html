DTEQ 02 · SPATIAL MAPPING

Everyday Play Infrastructure
in Hudson Square

Focusing on young children and caregivers, this project examines how Hudson Square supports everyday access to care, play, and public open space. Spatial analysis reveals how large blocks, traffic barriers, and waterfront-oriented open spaces can weaken a continuous child-supporting network.

Research Process

01

Growing Child Population

Manhattan Community District 2’s under-18 population increased from 7,936 in 2010 to 8,587 in 2020, an 8.2% increase—outpacing the district’s overall population growth.

NYC Department of City Planning · 2010/2020 Census. Total population: 90,016 → 92,445 (+2.7%).
02

Growing Pressure on Play Space

As families with young children increase, existing active play spaces face growing pressure. Large-scale development and rezoning are expected to further increase demand for parks and recreational space.

03

Limited Public Open Space

The need for additional public open space is already recognized as a district-wide concern. Within Hudson Square, this raises a more specific question: how well are existing parks, playgrounds, schools, and childcare facilities connected as an everyday network for children and caregivers?

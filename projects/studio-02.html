Studio 02 · The Standing Now of the Present

Reciprocity in the Village

Combining top-down spatial analysis with bottom-up ethnographic research, this project explores the invisible infrastructure of workers who sustain Greenwich Village. The zine reframes community as a network of everyday reciprocity rather than a place defined only by residency.

Research Process

01

GIS Mapping

GIS mapping revealed a sharp contrast between the neighborhood’s high household incomes and the many workers commuting in from elsewhere. We initially framed these service workers as an invisible population—essential to the area, yet unable to live close to where they worked or fully participate in the community they sustained.

02

Street Interviews

Street interviews challenged this initial assumption. Many workers spoke positively about the neighborhood, their jobs, and their everyday relationships with the people they encountered. Rather than describing only exclusion or dissatisfaction, their stories revealed a more complex and often positive connection to the Village.

03

Narrative Zine

This finding shifted the project from documenting invisibility to examining reciprocity. The final zine follows the daily interactions between residents and workers, portraying community as a mutually sustaining relationship shaped by care, service, familiarity, and repeated everyday encounters.
